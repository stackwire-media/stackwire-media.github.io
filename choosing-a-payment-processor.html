Flat-rate or interchange-plus: how to choose a payment processor

By Stackwire Editorial. Published 6 November 2026.

Your processor touches every sale, so a small difference in fees compounds fast. Here is how to compare options on what you will actually pay.

The two main pricing models

Flat-rate bundles everything into one price, commonly around 2.9% plus 30 cents for online cards. It is simple and quick to set up. Interchange-plus passes the card network’s fee through at cost and adds a fixed processor markup, so you can see exactly what you pay for. One 2026 guide puts competitive small-business markups at roughly 0.15% to 0.30% plus a few cents per transaction.

When each one wins

Flat-rate suits new or low-volume businesses. Several guides suggest interchange-plus usually becomes cheaper somewhere around $15,000 to $20,000 in monthly volume, because flat pricing charges you the same on cheap debit cards as on premium rewards cards.

A worked example (illustrative numbers)

Take $50,000 a month across 1,000 sales. At 2.9% plus $0.30, you pay $1,450 plus $300, or $1,750 (3.5%). On interchange-plus, assume an average interchange of 1.8% ($900), a 0.30% markup ($150) and $0.10 per sale ($100). That totals $1,150 (2.3%), a saving of about $600 a month. Your real interchange will depend on your card mix.

A checklist beyond the headline rate

  • Payout speed: standard payouts often take 1 to 2 business days, and instant payouts usually cost extra.
  • Account holds: ask how the processor handles reserves and fund freezes, a documented risk at higher volumes.
  • Chargeback fees and how disputes are handled.
  • Contract terms: monthly minimums, long contracts and cancellation fees.
  • Fit: marketplaces need split payouts, and SaaS needs solid subscription billing.

Fees change often. Confirm current pricing with each provider before you decide.
