Payment Gateway Fees: Rates, Charges, and Cost Examples

Payment gateway fees can range from a monthly charge plus a small fee per transaction to a percentage of every sale. The price depends on whether you use a standalone gateway or a provider that bundles checkout, processing, and other payment services.

For example, a standalone gateway may charge a monthly fee and a per-transaction fee while your merchant account provider charges separately for processing. An all-in-one provider may instead publish one rate that covers its standard online card-payment service. To compare costs fairly, add up every charge that applies to your setup—not just the line labeled “gateway fee.”

Below, we explain the common charges, compare published US prices, and show how to estimate a monthly cost.

What are payment gateway fees?

A payment gateway securely collects or transmits payment details from checkout to the services that authorize and process the transaction. Some businesses pay a separate gateway provider. Other businesses use a payment service provider (PSP) that bundles gateway functionality with processing and, in some cases, merchant-account services.

That difference affects how fees appear:

  • Standalone gateway: The gateway may charge its own monthly, per-transaction, or batch fees. Card processing and acquiring charges may come from separate providers.
  • Bundled payment platform: The provider may charge one percentage-plus-fixed fee for a successful online card payment, with no separate gateway line item on standard pricing.

The terms “gateway fee” and “payment processing fee” are sometimes used loosely in marketing. Read the provider’s pricing page and contract to see exactly which services the price includes. For a deeper explanation of the roles, see our guide to payment gateways vs. payment processors.

Common payment gateway and processing charges

Charge What it may cover What to check
Monthly or account fee Access to a standalone gateway or payment service Is the fee charged per account, location, or gateway?
Per-transaction fee Routing or processing each transaction Is it charged in addition to a percentage, or is it the full gateway-only charge? Do declines, voids, or refunds also incur it?
Percentage fee A share of each card transaction Does it include processing, acquiring, gateway access, or only some of these?
Batch fee Sending a group of authorized transactions for settlement Is it charged per batch, per day, or per transaction?
Setup or integration fee Initial configuration, migration, or custom development Is setup included, and are optional integrations billed separately?
International or currency fee Cross-border cards or converting currencies Does it apply on top of the regular card rate?
Dispute or chargeback fee Handling a disputed transaction Is it charged for every dispute, and can it be returned if the dispute is resolved in your favor?
Optional tools Fraud products, recurring billing, tokenization, or advanced reporting Which tools are included in the quoted plan and which are add-ons?

Not every provider charges every item. Some rates also vary by payment method, online versus in-person acceptance, plan, card origin, or negotiated pricing.

Example: estimate fees on $10,000 in monthly online card sales

Suppose a business makes 100 successful domestic online card transactions totaling $10,000 in one month. That is an average transaction of $100. Using only the published example rates above:

Example rate Calculation Estimated monthly charge in this example
Stripe standard domestic online cards 2.9% × $10,000 + $0.30 × 100 $320
Square Free online or invoice payments 3.3% × $10,000 + $0.30 × 100 $360
PayPal standard credit and debit cards 2.99% × $10,000 + $0.49 × 100 $348
Authorize.net Gateway Only $25 + ($0.10 × 100) + ($0.10 × 30 daily batches) $38 in gateway charges, plus separate processing and merchant-account fees

The example assumes one settled batch on each of 30 days and excludes refunds, disputes, international cards, currency conversion, optional services, taxes, plan subscriptions, and any separately billed processing or acquiring costs. The Authorize.net figure is not a total cost of accepting cards; it shows only its stated gateway charges under these assumptions. Your statement and contract determine your actual bill.

How to calculate your actual payment cost

Use a recent merchant statement and calculate the effective rate for the same period:

Effective rate = total payment-related fees ÷ total card sales × 100

For example, if you paid $350 in payment-related charges on $10,000 in card sales, your effective rate was 3.5%. Include the charges that apply to your business, such as gateway, processor, acquiring, network, cross-border, currency-conversion, and dispute charges. Keep optional software or hardware costs separate if you want to compare transaction pricing alone, then add them back when comparing total operating cost.

To get a like-for-like quote, give each provider the same information:

  1. Monthly card volume and number of transactions.
  2. Average order size and typical card types.
  3. Share of transactions that are international, manually entered, recurring, or disputed.
  4. Required payment methods, checkout integrations, fraud tools, and reporting.
  5. Expected settlement timing and the countries where you sell.

Ask for an itemized schedule showing monthly charges, per-transaction fees, percentage fees, batches, disputes, international payments, currency conversion, and optional products. Then compare the total against your own sales mix. A low gateway-only rate can still lead to a higher overall cost if separate processing and account fees apply.

Frequently asked questions

How much do payment gateway fees cost?

There is no single rate. A standalone gateway may charge a monthly fee plus transaction or batch charges, with processing billed separately. A bundled provider may charge a percentage and fixed amount per successful payment instead. Compare the complete schedule for your plan and payment mix.

Is a payment gateway fee the same as a processing fee?

Not necessarily. A gateway fee can cover the technology that securely routes checkout data. A processing fee covers the services involved in authorizing and handling a payment. Some providers bundle these services, so their published card rate may not be a gateway-only fee.

Can a payment gateway be free?

Some bundled providers advertise no monthly gateway or setup charge on a standard plan, but they may still charge transaction fees and other applicable costs. Check the terms for your specific payment method, plan, and business.

Why can two businesses pay different rates?

Rates can depend on provider plan, sales volume, average transaction size, card mix, in-person versus online acceptance, international payments, risk, and negotiated terms. A price shown for one plan may not apply to another.

What should I compare besides the headline rate?

Compare all monthly and per-transaction charges, settlement timing, supported payment methods, dispute handling, international and currency fees, contract terms, integration requirements, and the provider’s support. Use your own recent transaction data to estimate the total.

Bottom line

“Payment gateway fees” can describe a standalone technology charge or be used informally for the full cost of accepting payments. Check whether the quote includes processing and acquiring, then compare itemized costs using your own sales volume and transaction mix. That gives you a more useful number than the headline rate alone.
